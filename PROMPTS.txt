THE SHRINKAGE COUNTER — the exact prompts from the episode, word for word.
Paste them into your AI assistant in order. Prompt 1 builds the first version; prompts 2 and 3
are the fixes from the episode — they exist because the first two versions were wrong in ways
worth understanding before you trust any tool with your inventory.

================================ PROMPT 1 ================================

Build me a single HTML file I can open in my browser, no internet, no account, that tells me
what inventory walked out of my store.

What it's for: I run a small retail shop. Twice a year we count everything on the shelves. I
have the count sheet and I have the movement ledger between the counts — sales, deliveries,
customer returns, and damaged stock we wrote off. I want to know which products are missing
units that nothing in the ledger explains, and what those units cost me.

Data in: two pastes.
1) The count sheet: CSV with columns SKU, Item, Unit Cost, First Count, First Date,
   Second Count, Second Date.
2) The movements between the counts: CSV with columns Date, Type, SKU, Qty, Ref — where Type
   is sale, received, return, or writeoff, and Ref is the receipt or delivery reference.

What I want out: for every product, what the second count SHOULD have been given the first
count and the movements, what it actually was, and the difference — in units and in dollars at
my cost. Total it up. Show me the worst offenders first. Show your arithmetic, not just a
verdict.

================================ PROMPT 2 ================================

The first version poured the whole store into one bucket and told me my inventory was 95.7%
accurate. That number is useless: a surplus on one shelf cancelled missing units on another,
so four gallons of paint that appeared from nowhere gave my missing drills an alibi. Rebuild
the rules:

- EVERY PRODUCT KEEPS ITS OWN LEDGER. A drill that's missing is missing, no matter what the
  paint aisle did. Never net one product's surplus against another product's loss.
- Value each product's variance at ITS OWN unit cost. No averages, ever.
- A surplus is its own finding — usually a delivery keyed wrong — listed separately, never
  used to shrink the missing total.
- A movement whose SKU is not on the count sheet is NOT silently created or skipped — flag it
  loudly: this product sells but was never counted.

================================ PROMPT 3 ================================

Now make it trustworthy enough to accuse a shelf of theft.

- All arithmetic in integer units and integer cents. No floating-point dollars anywhere.
- A count window per product: only movements dated AFTER the first count and ON OR BEFORE the
  second count touch its ledger. A delivery that arrived the day after the second count
  belongs to the next audit, not this one. Refuse out-of-window rows with the reason printed.
- A reference number seen twice is HELD, not applied — one delivery must never count double.
  List every held row.
- Refuse what you can't read, out loud, with the line number: a date that doesn't exist, a
  quantity that isn't a positive whole number, a movement type you don't know, a count or cost
  that isn't a number. Never guess.
- A written-off unit is an EXPLAINED loss. It never counts as shrink, and it is never billed
  twice. Give me a switch to include or exclude recorded write-offs from the headline number.
- Two more dials: how many units of count noise to forgive per product, and a unit-cost
  spotlight for the expensive stuff worth walking out with.
- The page audits itself on every run: rows in = applied + refused + orphaned + held; the
  verdicts re-derived by an independent second pass and compared; and if too many rows are
  refused, print DO NOT TRUST THIS AUDIT across the page and withhold the findings entirely.
  A confident answer from mangled data is worse than no answer.

=========================== THE SECOND OPINION ===========================

The episode also used an independent checker — the same rules implemented again in Python by a
separate session, sharing no code with the page — run on the same two files. checker.py is in
this folder. That second opinion is what caught the sixteen-drill false accusation, the
double-counted delivery, and the write-offs billed twice in version two. When two
implementations disagree, one of them is wrong, and it is worth finding out which before you
accuse anybody of anything.
